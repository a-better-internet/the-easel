An independent, unofficial homage to the Music Easel's 208C sound source, built for no-input playing. Not affiliated with Buchla USA.

Playing an Easel that plays itself

Nothing here is played from a keyboard. Every sound comes from self-oscillation, self-clocking, and signals the Easel routes back into itself. Turn on power, pick a patch, then change one thing at a time.

Reading the panel

  • Black banana jacks are inputs. Coloured banana jacks are outputs, coloured by source: blue sequencer, orange envelope, yellow pulser, white random, green mod osc out, blue inverter and envelope-detector outputs. Sources repeat across the jack field so one is always next to a CV input.
  • Audio uses the small silver Tini-Jax jacks: f.m. in, m.osc, c.osc, gate1 in, aux in / nse, gate2 sig and master out.
  • Each modulated parameter has a CV slider beside its main slider, with its black input jack underneath. The CV slider sets how much of whatever you patch in reaches the parameter.
  • Times are printed .002 s at the top to 10 s at the bottom: sliders (and positive CV) push things faster.

Patching

  • Drag from any jack to any other jack. West-coast rules: anything can go anywhere.
  • Patch two neighbouring banana jacks and you get a shorting bar, the quickest way to send a source into the CV input beside it.
  • Banana stacking: drop a cable on a plug that's already in a jack and it stacks on top. The new plug inherits that jack, so stacking is how you send one output to many places, or mix several outputs into one input.
  • Outputs that share a stack (a net) mix passively. Each input on the net hears that mix.
  • Drag a plug to pull it out and move it. Drop it on empty panel to remove the cable. Alt/Option-drag a plug to start a new cable stacked on it.
  • Shift-click a plug, or hold Shift and click a cable, to delete it. Double-tap a plug on touch screens.
  • Click a jack, then another to patch without dragging. With the keyboard, Tab to a jack, press Enter, then Enter on the destination. Delete pulls the top plug.

A loop is positive when the return pushes the same way (it runs to an extreme, then saturates or locks), and negative when it opposes the change (it settles and hovers). Patching the return through the inverter flips one into the other. The most musical results usually sit near the boundary.

Gain staging & safety

  • Start silent. Keep the monitor level low and bring loop gain (channel B, master) up from zero. Audio re-entry loops can jump from silence to full level.
  • Use headphones with care while setting up audio loops. A runaway loop can hold a piercing tone.
  • Find the point where a loop starts, then back off slightly. The most interesting zone is just below where it locks.
  • If a control loop runs away, reach for the inverter first, then reduce the mod depth.
  • master volume sets the level at the master out jacks, so it is part of any loop you patch from there. monitor level is what you hear.
  • reset clears every oscillator, filter and loop state. What you hear always passes a DC blocker and a limiter.

Recording

  • rec records a 24-bit WAV at the audio device's sample rate (usually 44.1 or 48 kHz), mono like the instrument. Press it again to stop; the file downloads, and stays linked beside the button until your next take.
  • Record from master out (the default): the line-level mix, DC-blocked, unaffected by the monitor knob and never touched by the output limiter. Set the level with master volume and watch the peak read-out. Or record from monitor: exactly what you hear, limiter included.
  • Audio is captured inside the engine, sample for sample, before the browser's output stage, and converted to 24 bits with TPDF dither.
  • Turning the power off stops the take and saves it. A take can run for hours (the WAV format tops out near 4 GB, about 8 hours at 48 kHz).
